# Wan 3.0 Pricing Guide: Alibaba vs OpenRouter vs DeepInfra

Published on 2026.10.20 by DeepInfra

![Wan 3.0 Pricing Guide: Alibaba vs OpenRouter vs DeepInfra](https://deepinfrabldev.wpenginepowered.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/wan-pricing-guide.png)

[Wan3.0](https://deepinfra.com/Wan-AI/Wan3.0-Video) costs very different amounts depending on where you buy it. Alibaba Cloud, the model’s creator, lists it from $0.041256 per second outside Singapore. OpenRouter lists it from $0.0425 per second at 480p. DeepInfra lists Wan3.0-Video at $0.20 per second and accepts reference images, video, audio, files, and web pages.

Alibaba and OpenRouter are the cheaper entry points. DeepInfra’s broader request schema is easier to justify for multimodal, document-heavy workflows.

## **Wan3.0 Executive Summary**

Alibaba Cloud prices Wan3.0 by region. Its Singapore listing is $0.05, $0.10, and $0.20 per second at 480p, 720p, and 1080p. Its US, Germany, Japan, and China regions list a flat $0.041256, $0.082513, and $0.165025. OpenRouter lists Wan 3.0 from $0.0425 per second at 480p, which implies about $0.085 at 720p and $0.17 at 1080p under the same 15% discount. DeepInfra lists a single rate of $0.20 per second regardless of tier.

Comparing generations before you commit also matters. OpenRouter lists Wan 2.6 text-to-video from $0.04 per second, and DeepInfra’s Wan2.6 text-to-video model costs $0.10 per second.

## **How Wan3.0 Is Billed**

Video models do not follow the [token pricing](https://deepinfra.com/blog/pricing-101-token-math-cost-per-completion) that language models use. You buy seconds of generated video, and the price changes with provider packaging and resolution.

### **What drives the bill**

- The number of seconds you generate
- The resolution tier you choose, where most providers price tiers separately
- The provider and region you use
- The number of regenerations you need when a clip misses the target

| **Billing factor** | **What it is** | **Why it matters** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Output video seconds | The duration of the generated clip | This is the main cost driver across all three providers |
| Resolution tier | 480p, 720p, or 1080p output | Alibaba’s and OpenRouter’s rates scale with resolution, so drafts at 480p cost a fraction of final renders |
| Region | Which Alibaba Cloud region serves the request | Singapore prices roughly 20% above the other five listed regions |
| Prompt and media inputs | Text, images, reference video, reference audio, files, and links | None of the three listings shows a separate token charge for inputs |
| Regenerations | Extra paid generations after prompt misses or quality problems | A lower rate can still cost more if clips need frequent reruns |

## **Practical Cost Guidance by Provider**

### **Alibaba Cloud suits the lowest list price**

Outside Singapore, Alibaba’s own rates undercut both OpenRouter and DeepInfra at every resolution tier. Teams that can route requests to the US, Germany, Japan, or China regions get the cheapest list price with no intermediary.

### **OpenRouter suits the cheapest routed second**

Among the gateway options, OpenRouter has the lowest listed rate at 480p and publishes latency and availability data, which helps with cost-controlled iteration on prompts.

### **DeepInfra suits pipeline-heavy workloads**

If one endpoint can take a reference image, reference audio, a file, or a web page, you need fewer surrounding services. The per-second rate is higher, but the total workflow cost can still be lower.

### **Resolution discipline matters**

Testing prompts at 1080p turns small experiments into budget items. A safer pattern looks like this:

- Draft at 480p when possible.
- Validate motion and content.
- Rerun only the shortlisted clips at higher resolution.

The saving depends on the provider. Alibaba’s and OpenRouter’s rates drop with resolution, while DeepInfra lists a single rate, so a 480p draft on DeepInfra saves time but not money.

### **Regenerations are the hidden cost**

OpenRouter’s published latency and availability help you estimate the cost of unstable runs. Every miss costs generation time, and every resubmitted job delays the pipeline.

### **Input complexity changes the economics**

Teams that pass documents, URLs, audio references, and mixed media gain from DeepInfra’s broader request schema, which can remove glue code. Teams that send prompts and an occasional first-frame image can defend Alibaba’s or OpenRouter’s lower rate more easily.

### **Treat token cost as render cost**

With language models, cost follows prompt and completion length. With Wan3.0, the closer mental model is rendering. Every extra second, every higher resolution, and every unnecessary rerun adds to the bill.

## **DeepInfra for Wan3.0**

DeepInfra prices Wan-AI/Wan3.0-Video at $0.20 per second of generated video. It positions the model as a premium and flexible video endpoint, not a commodity render API.

The platform runs on bare-metal infrastructure in US data centers and is SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certified. A zero-retention policy covers inputs and outputs.

DeepInfra also hosts lower-priced video models for lighter jobs, including [Wan2.6 text-to-video](https://deepinfra.com/Wan-AI/Wan2.6-T2V) at $0.10 per second and [Google Veo 3.1 Fast](https://deepinfra.com/google/veo-3.1-fast) at $0.15 per second. A [comparison of DeepInfra and Alibaba pricing](https://deepinfra.com/blog/qwen3-8-27b-pricing-deepinfra-vs-alibaba) for a language model shows how the two platforms differ on a shared model.

## **Real-World Cost Scenarios for Developers**

Each scenario uses DeepInfra’s $0.20 per second rate. OpenRouter figures use its $0.17 (1080p) and $0.0425 (480p) per-second rates. Alibaba Cloud figures use its non-Singapore 1080p rate of $0.165025 per second.

### **Scenario 1: URL-to-video explainer pipeline**

An internal tool turns product pages, docs, and launch URLs into short explainer clips. Wan3.0 on DeepInfra can parse web pages, files, and complex images directly, so many requests skip a separate extraction layer.

- Volume: 200 videos per month at 10 seconds each, or 2,000 output seconds
- DeepInfra: 2,000 seconds at $0.20 per second equals $400
- OpenRouter: $340 at 1080p, or $85 at 480p
- Alibaba Cloud: $330.05 at 1080p outside Singapore, or $400 in Singapore

This scenario fits apps that accept raw links, uploaded briefs, or mixed reference assets from users.

### **Scenario 2: Character-consistent marketing video**

A team needs recurring short videos with consistent characters, style, and sound cues across campaigns. DeepInfra supports reference images, reference video, reference audio, and editing workflows through one endpoint.

- Volume: 120 videos per month at 15 seconds each, or 1,800 output seconds
- DeepInfra: 1,800 seconds at $0.20 per second equals $360
- OpenRouter: $306 at 1080p, or $76.50 at 480p
- Alibaba Cloud: $297.05 at 1080p outside Singapore, or $360 in Singapore

The higher DeepInfra rate pays off when consistency matters more than the cheapest draft.

### **Scenario 3: Creative tooling with reproducible outputs**

A developer-facing video API or creative copilot needs controllable reruns. DeepInfra exposes seed, duration, resolution, watermark, and prompt extension controls for repeatable regeneration.

- Volume: 500 videos per month at 5 seconds each, or 2,500 output seconds
- DeepInfra: 2,500 seconds at $0.20 per second equals $500
- OpenRouter: $425 at 1080p, or $106.25 at 480p
- Alibaba Cloud: $412.56 at 1080p outside Singapore, or $500 in Singapore

This scenario suits SaaS products where developers want standardized generation settings.

### **Scenario 4: Media-rich asset transformation service**

A service accepts customer media packs that include a brief as a file, a reference clip, a voice cue, and a set of images. DeepInfra handles reference_audio, reference_video, reference_image, file, and link inputs in one model interface.

- Volume: 80 videos per month at 20 seconds each, or 1,600 output seconds
- DeepInfra: 1,600 seconds at $0.20 per second equals $320
- OpenRouter: $272 at 1080p, or $68 at 480p
- Alibaba Cloud: $264.04 at 1080p outside Singapore, or $320 in Singapore

The higher DeepInfra rate is easiest to justify here, because it replaces preprocessing steps and format handoffs between services.

### **Scenario 5: Production batch generation with audio**

A steady monthly batch of short clips with generated audio needs one endpoint that can take prompt-only jobs today and richer media-guided jobs later. DeepInfra’s default audio and broad input design simplify that migration.

- Volume: 300 videos per month at 8 seconds each, or 2,400 output seconds
- DeepInfra: 2,400 seconds at $0.20 per second equals $480
- OpenRouter: $408 at 1080p, or $102 at 480p
- Alibaba Cloud: $396.06 at 1080p outside Singapore, or $480 in Singapore

This scenario suits teams that expect to move from prompt-based generation to reference-heavy generation.

### **What the scenarios show**

Alibaba Cloud’s non-Singapore regions list the lowest cost at 1080p in every scenario, since there is no intermediary markup. DeepInfra’s strength with Wan3.0 is input range and production controls, not the lowest price per second.

Apps that need only plain text-to-video or simple image-to-video can defend Alibaba’s or OpenRouter’s rate on render cost alone. Apps that need reference audio, reference video, file parsing, web page ingestion, editing, replication, or driving often find DeepInfra more practical despite the higher rate.

A split workflow shows the gap. Take 100 drafts of 10 seconds and 10 finals of 10 seconds. Running everything on DeepInfra uses 1,100 seconds and costs $220. Drafting on OpenRouter at 480p costs $42.50 for 1,000 seconds, and 100 final seconds on DeepInfra cost $20, for a total of $62.50.

## **Conclusion**

The core decision with Wan3.0 depends on more than price per second. It depends on the workflow assumptions built into the provider. Alibaba Cloud and OpenRouter offer lower entry rates, and OpenRouter publishes latency and availability data, which helps when you iterate fast and cost leads. DeepInfra offers a richer input surface and production controls, which matter once a pipeline moves beyond prompt-plus-image into reference video, audio, files, or web pages. Broader [inference costs at scale](https://deepinfra.com/blog/inference-economics-ai-costs-at-scale) follow the same logic.

Weigh four factors before you commit: input complexity, expected regeneration rate, resolution discipline, and whether going direct to Alibaba Cloud fits your region and rate-limit needs. Defaulting to 1080p for every draft shifts the economics quickly on any provider.

If your workflow is ready for production and needs the full range of reference inputs, DeepInfra’s Wan-AI/Wan3.0-Video is a practical starting point. If your inputs are simpler and price leads, compare Alibaba’s own regional rates against OpenRouter before you commit.
