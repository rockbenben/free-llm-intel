# The attendee’s guide to the AWS re:Invent 2026 AI/ML track

From November 30th to December 4th, Amazon Web Services (AWS) will hold its annual premier learning event: [re:Invent](https://aws.amazon.com/events/reinvent/). At this event, attendees will become stronger and more proficient in various areas of AWS technology through a variety of experiences: keynotes given by AWS leaders, interactive working sessions led by AWS experts, and [fun, immersive activities such as live entertainment and games at re:Play](https://aws.amazon.com/events/reinvent/experiences/uniquely-reinvent/).

There are over [2,000 learning sessions](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=) that focus on specific topics at various skill levels, and the AI/ML (AIM) track has more than [140 unique sessions](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM) for you to choose from. We’re here to help you identify the sessions that best fit your needs. Even if you can’t join in person, you can catch up with many of the sessions on demand and even watch the keynotes live.

## The basics: Session types

If you can join us, then remember that we offer several types of sessions that can help maximize your learning in a variety of AWS topics.

re:Invent attendees can choose to attend breakouts, chalk talks, builder sessions, workshops, and code talks. The breakout sessions are recorded and uploaded a few days after to the [AWS Events YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@AWSEventsChannel), while the rest are live non-recorded interactive sessions. Here are the details for each session type:

- **Breakout sessions:** Attendees are in a lecture-style 60-minute informative session presented by AWS experts, customers, and partners.
- **Chalk-talk sessions:** Attendees interact with presenters, asking questions, and using a whiteboard in session.
- **Builder sessions:** Attendees participate in a one-hour session and build something.
- **Workshop sessions:** Attendees join a two-hour interactive session where you work in a small team to solve a real problem using AWS services.
- **Code talk sessions:** Attendees participate in engaging code-focused sessions where an expert leads a live coding session.
- **Lightning talk sessions:** Attendees watch a 20-minute demo dedicated to either a specific service or customer story (located in the Venetian Expo Hall or Wynn/Encore Upper Convention Promenade).

With so many sessions to pick from, it helps to know how the pieces fit together. Here is how AWS thinks about its AI offerings, so you can decide where to begin and where to focus.

## The AWS AI stack overview and tie-in to re:Invent sessions

AWS offers a comprehensive AI portfolio in the cloud. It covers everything from the physical infrastructure and AI chips that power AI workloads up through ready-to-use agentic solutions that teams can adopt today. Understanding how these services work together helps you navigate re:Invent and focus on the sessions most relevant to what you’re building.

![AWS AI stack diagram showing layers from global infrastructure and AI chips up through models, agents, and ready-to-use agentic solutions](https://d2908q01vomqb2.cloudfront.net/f1f836cb4ea6efb2a0b1b99f41ad8b103eff4b59/2026/11/10/ML-22321-1-2.png)

Figure 1: The AWS AI stack

**[Global Infrastructure](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/global-infrastructure/)** – It all starts with data centers designed and optimized for AI workloads. These facilities are built to handle the power, cooling, and networking demands that large AI training and inference jobs require.

**AI Chips –** Running inside those data centers are two compute options: [AWS Trainium](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/machine-learning/trainium/), purpose-built for high-performance AI training and inference, and [NVIDIA GPUs](https://aws.amazon.com/nvidia/) for teams that need GPU-based acceleration. Customers choose based on their workload, performance requirements, and cost targets.

**Data Foundation –** AI is only as good as the data behind it. [AWS Context](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/context-intelligence-for-your-data-and-ai-agents-at-scale/) connects your data across storage, databases, analytics, and search services, giving models and agents access to the right information at the right time.

**Models, Training, and Inferencing –** This is where models get built, trained, fine-tuned, and served. [Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/) gives you managed access to [hundreds of leading models](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/model-choice/) from multiple providers through a unified API. [Amazon SageMaker AI](https://aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/ai/) gives you full control to train, fine-tune, and deploy custom models on dedicated infrastructure. Many customers use both depending on the workload.

**Agent Development and Management –** [Amazon Bedrock AgentCore](https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/agentcore/) is where you build, deploy, and manage AI agents. It supports any model or provider mix, including models from both Amazon Bedrock and SageMaker AI, so teams can design agents that pick the right model for each task without being locked into a single provider.

**Agentic Solutions –** At the top of the stack sit purpose-built agents that are ready to use. [Kiro](https://kiro.dev/) helps developers ship correctly the first time with spec-driven development. [AWS Quick](https://aws.amazon.com/quick/) connects your applications, tools, and data into a personal knowledge graph that learns how you work. [Amazon Connect](https://aws.amazon.com/products/connect/) delivers agents for customer experience, hiring, supply chain, and healthcare. [AWS Marketplace](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/) gives you access to partner-built AI solutions.

**Security, Governance, and Cost Optimization** run across every layer of the AI stack. They are built into the foundation, with hardware-level isolation at the chip layer and controls that enforce what agents can access and do.

The AI-focused sessions at re:Invent span the full stack. Whether you’re evaluating AI chips, serving models in production, building custom models, deploying agents, or adopting ready-to-use solutions, this guide helps you find the sessions that matter most. Use it to build a smarter re:Invent schedule.

The artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) track primarily focuses on three core layers of this stack: **AI chips**, **models, training, and inference**, and **agent development and management**, with **security, governance, and cost optimization** woven throughout. The following sections start at the chip layer and move up through models and agents. Then come sessions that tie the stack together. The guide ends with other relevant topics, such as sustainability and infrastructure where you run your AI workloads. Within each section, sessions for newcomers come first and technical deep dives come last.

## AI chips: NVIDIA GPUs and AWS Trainium

AWS gives you two main choices of AI chips for training and inference: NVIDIA GPUs and AWS Trainium. Start with the one you use today, then explore the other.

AWS offers a wide range of Amazon Elastic Compute Cloud (Amazon EC2) instances with NVIDIA GPUs. These sessions show how to run AI training and inference on them securely and reliably.

Start with [AIM2210](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM2210) for the latest on GPU instances. In [AIM3301](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM3301), a customer shares how it gets the most from its GPU capacity by optimizing GPU scheduling. [AIM392](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM392) explains when EC2 G7e and G7 instances are a good fit in place of P-series instances. Want to tune AI infrastructure with self-managed agents? Join [AIM346](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM346).

If you prefer hands-on learning, try [AIM345](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM345) on disaggregated inference serving. In [AIM344](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM344), you find the cause of real GPU cluster failures and how to recover from them. In the [AIM347](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM347) workshop, you can practice GPU performance engineering.

### AWS Trainium sessions

AWS Trainium is a family of AI chips purpose-built for next-generation deep learning training and inference. It delivers high performance at lower cost. Trainium runs on AWS Neuron, the software stack that connects Trainium to familiar frameworks like PyTorch, so teams build without rewrites. Customers who adopt Trainium-based Amazon EC2 instances may see better price performance than comparable instances.

If you’re new to Trainium, [AIM211](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM211) is the place to start. It traces the full stack, beginning with the silicon and working up to the application. For the latest system, [AIM310](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM310) shows how Trainium3 and the Trn3 UltraServer keep large mixture-of-experts models performant. If you’re still evaluating transitioning to Trainium3, the [AIM2213](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM2213) panel gathers four AI leaders who have already shipped real products on Trainium.

Several sessions are built around specific roles. Scientists and researchers who live in PyTorch should bring their questions to [AIM339](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM339), a nanochat code talk, before getting hands-on in the [AIM334](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM334) distributed-training workshop. ML engineers and developers can architect a production AI assistant with Hugging Face models in [AIM349](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM349), and get hands-on with inference scaling in the [AIM326](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM326) workshop. Performance engineers chasing peak throughput will want [AIM329](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM329) on agentic optimization loops, followed by [INV511](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=INV511), a 500-level look inside the Neuron Kernel Interface for serious kernel designers. If you want to run Trainium on Amazon Elastic Kubernetes Service (Amazon EKS), [AIM311](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM311) whiteboards a resiliency stack that holds up across deployments as large as twenty thousand accelerators. If you deploy through SageMaker AI instead, see the [SageMaker AI section](https://aws.amazon.com/cn/blogs/machine-learning/the-attendees-guide-to-the-aws-reinvent-2026-ai-ml-track/#amazon-sagemaker-ai-sessions).

For those who want to go further, world models are an emerging class that power simulation in gaming, robotics, and autonomous driving. [AIM323](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM323) is a good starting point and covers serving world models with native PyTorch. From there, pick a hands-on path. In [AIM304](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM304) you will deploy and optimize a real-time video world model using agentic Neuron workflows, while in [AIM305](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM305) you will train vision-language-action models for physical AI. ## Amazon SageMaker AI sessions

Amazon SageMaker AI is the AWS service for customizing and deploying your own models. It handles the whole model lifecycle in one place. You can prepare data and customize a model on serverless compute. When you need more power, you can grow training jobs to thousands of accelerators. After your model is ready, deploy it on SageMaker inference endpoints or [SageMaker HyperPod](https://aws.amazon.com/sagemaker/ai/hyperpod/). You keep the model weights, and you decide where your models run.

## Build and run AI applications and agents with Amazon Bedrock

The model and inference approach you choose shapes what your AI app can do for users. Amazon Bedrock gives you a wide choice of models and helps you pick the right one for each task. It gives you the performance, security, and cost control to run AI apps in production. Some apps need to use tools and data, remember context, and act for you. For those, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore helps you build, connect, and improve agents.

New to Amazon Bedrock? Start with [AIM220](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM220). It covers what’s new in models, inference, security, and cost management. Then build and deploy your first Amazon Bedrock app in the hands-on [AIM317](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM317) workshop.

Next, think about the kind of workload you need to serve. A customer support app may need fast answers. A large document processing job may care more about cost and can wait for results. [AIM448](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM448) helps you pick the Amazon Bedrock inference option that fits your needs. It weighs performance, capacity, cost, and data residency. If your app must reason across your company’s information, attend [AIM308](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM308). In this session, you’ll build a knowledge assistant that finds answers across documents, images, structured data, and the links between them.

Your inference plan also needs to stay affordable as usage grows. [AIM437](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM437) helps you decide when to cache repeated context, route requests between models, or move work to batch inference. Model choice matters too. The newest model isn’t always the right one, and switching can change quality, speed, cost, and behavior. [AIM438](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM438) shows how to test candidate models on your own use cases so you can switch with confidence. After your app is live, [AIM374](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM374) covers the metrics, alarms, and operating habits to bring into production. Systems specialists can go deeper in [INV517](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=INV517). This 500-level session looks at the scheduling and parallelism methods behind efficient large-model inference.

If you want to build an agent, start with the full production picture rather than building infrastructure one piece at a time. [AIM212](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM212) introduces the complete AgentCore lifecycle. Nasdaq also shares how it runs agents in a regulated industry. For a hands-on path, try [AIM439](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM439). You start with a basic customer support agent. Then you add memory, tool access, identity, policies, observability, and evaluation until it’s ready for production. Ready to design a multi-agent system? [AIM407](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM407) uses [Strands Agents](https://strandsagents.com/) to coordinate specialized agents. You share state, handle failures, and deploy the result to AgentCore.

After your agent is running, look at what happens under the hood. Each planning step, retrieval, tool call, and retry can add another model call. That changes cost, speed, and reliability. [AIM429](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM429) connects agent design to inference behavior. It helps you think through context, harnesses, goals, and when an agent should stop. Developers who want to see these tradeoffs in code should follow it with [AIM454](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM454). That session builds function calling, multi-step reasoning, and retrieval live.

The stakes rise as agents serve more users and reach more systems. Bring your scaling questions to [AIM436](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM436), where you work through traffic spikes, tool failures, model timeouts, and multi-tenancy. Platform teams that manage agents across a company should attend [AIM303](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM303). It covers how to find agents, control tool access, manage identity, and see your whole fleet. If your priority is securing every step between a user request and an agent action, pair it with [AIM370](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM370). Then use [AIM440](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM440) to turn production traces and evaluations into a steady way to improve agent quality.

Finally, pick a session that looks like the work you want an agent to do. [AIM340](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM340) follows PepsiCo’s conversational ordering agent. It shows how memory, company APIs, and controls work together in a customer-facing workflow. [AIM369](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM369) focuses on agents that respond to business events and run processes to the end, with human approval where needed. Software teams can get hands-on in [AIM416](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM416). ## Building trust in AI agents

AI agents now do real work, like approving claims, issuing refunds, and acting on customer data. Teams need a way to trust what those agents do and prove it to others. That starts with [responsible AI](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/responsible-ai/), which helps organizations make careful choices about how AI systems are designed, built, and run. AWS builds on that with [Trust in Depth](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/trust-in-depth-giving-agents-more-autonomy-without-losing-control/). It’s an approach for giving agents more freedom without losing control.

If you’re new to AI trust, start with the [AIM2216](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM2216) panel. It covers how to build confidence at each stage of an agent’s life. Topics include keeping an agent limited to its job and seeing exactly what it did. To see responsible AI governance in practice, attend [AIM391](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM391). It shows how John Deere used AI Risk Intelligence to replace periodic manual reviews with continuous, automated governance. The change cut assessment time by more than 30 percent.

Several sessions are built for specific roles. Developers can join the [AIM336](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM336) workshop to audit a multi-agent claims system for responsible AI gaps. Then they fix what they find and write a report backed by evidence. Risk, compliance, and data science teams can join [AIM332](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM332). This chalk talk covers testing whether agents treat people fairly when they approve loans or issue refunds. You learn how to choose fairness metrics and catch bias in outcomes that affect people’s lives. Do your agents decide things like insurance eligibility? The [AIM335](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM335) workshop shows how to turn the rules behind those decisions into Automated Reasoning checks in Amazon Bedrock Guardrails. Each decision can then be proven correct and audited.

For a deeper look at the science, several expert sessions cover automated reasoning. [INV508](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=INV508) shows how subject matter experts can turn legal and regulatory policies into rules a machine can check. No coding is needed, and those rules then serve as AI guardrails. [INV526](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=INV526) looks at what can be proven about agent behavior today, and where the limits are. The [AIM451](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM451) chalk talk shows how to enforce rules that agents can’t get around. It also shows how to keep people involved in decisions machines shouldn’t make alone.

### AWS Sustainability

AWS is continuously innovating across its data centers, chips, and AI services to be more efficient and sustainable. AWS also gives you the data and tools to measure, track, and reduce the environmental impact of your own cloud and AI workloads running on AWS. These sessions discuss how we’re making our infrastructure more efficient and show how to act on sustainability data while still meeting your business goals. For the latest on the AWS Sustainability service, attend [AIM201](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM201). This lightning talk covers new impact areas beyond carbon, more detailed data, and API access. In [AIM205](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM205), learn how AWS makes AI infrastructure more sustainable. It uses hardware with low embodied carbon, energy-efficient custom chips, and data centers tuned by machine learning (ML). Want to understand the method behind your carbon data? Join the [AIM321](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM321) chalk talk. It shows how to use that data to build AI workloads with a smaller footprint, which often cost less too.

If you prefer hands-on learning, try [AIM320](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM320). ### Local Zones and AWS AI Factories

As teams move AI into production, where a workload runs matters as much as how it’s built. Large training jobs, fine-tuning, and real-time inference each need different things from infrastructure. AWS gives you several places to run them: [AWS Regions](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/global-infrastructure/), [AWS Local Zones](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/global-infrastructure/localzones/), [AWS Dedicated Local Zones](https://aws.amazon.com/dedicatedlocalzones/), [AWS AI Factories](https://aws.amazon.com/about-aws/global-infrastructure/ai-factories/), and [AWS Outposts](https://aws.amazon.com/outposts/). Start with [HMC303](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=HMC303). It compares these options on the four factors that drive the choice: latency, data sovereignty, compute density, and scale. Databricks shares how it uses GPU capacity in select Local Zones. T-Mobile covers latency-sensitive distributed inference.

In [HMC307](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=HMC307), see how Netflix supports its visual effects and animation teams from the Los Angeles Local Zone. Netflix starts Amazon EC2 G7e instances with NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPUs just in time. That way, artists get ComfyUI environments when they need them.

Two hands-on workshops take different paths to building agents at the edge. [HMC402](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=HMC402) takes the managed route. You connect Amazon Bedrock AgentCore agents in an AWS Region to tools and agents in hybrid and edge locations. You use Model Context Protocol (MCP) and agent-to-agent (A2A) communication. [HMC403](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=HMC403) takes the self-managed path. You design, set up, and tune your own agents across Local Zones and Outposts. Then learn how to orchestrate fractional GPUs across the edge with NVIDIA Run:ai in the [HMC401](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=HMC401) code talk.

Some organizations can’t move AI workloads to the public cloud at all. Data sovereignty, data gravity, or latency can stand in the way. [AIM206](https://registration.awsevents.com/flow/awsevents/reinvent2026/eventcatalog/page/eventcatalog?search=AIM206) shows how AWS AI Factories bring AWS and NVIDIA AI infrastructure into your own data center. AWS runs and updates it for you. See how organizations run leading foundation models on premises for training and inference, while keeping full control of their data.

A chip wall shows every generation of AWS Graviton, AWS Trainium, AWS Inferentia, and AWS Nitro Cards. Touchscreens explain how these chips work with partner chips for agentic AI, training, and inference. Get up close to Trainium and Graviton server trays and racks, and learn how they scale out with Elastic Fabric Adapter (EFA) and Amazon EC2 UltraClusters. Then explore a 3D model of AWS AI Factories to see how AWS brings its infrastructure into your own data center. It’s a good stop after the AI chips, AWS AI Factories, and Local Zones sessions.
