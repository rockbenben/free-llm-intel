# Run your whole selling business in AWS Quick

Most sellers do not need another AI product to learn. They need the tools and information they already use brought together, so they can see what is happening in their business and act on it.

AWS Quick and the Amazon Selling Partner plugin do that together. Quick brings an Amazon seller’s sales data, inventory signals, and marketplace intelligence into one place, where questions become answers and answers become actions. It understands the seller’s full business context, so the seller does not need to re-explain their catalog, their margins, or their competitive landscape in every conversation. It is built on AWS, the same infrastructure Amazon uses to run its own marketplace.

At Amazon Accelerate on September 23, 2026, Amazon announced the Selling Partner plugin and launched it in beta in AWS Quick. This post covers what launched, how to claim the free AWS Quick offer, and what the experience looks like day to day. It also covers how the skills and connectors work when a seller puts them to use.

# 1. Why Quick is right for Amazon sellers

Four things make Quick a fit for how Amazon sellers already work: it learns the business, it reaches beyond the Amazon store, it turns data into decisions, and it protects seller data on infrastructure sellers already trust.

## Quick knows your business, not only your prompts

Quick and the Selling Partner plugin connect to a seller’s inventory feeds, sales data, and operational documents. From these, Quick builds a living understanding of the catalog structure, margin thresholds, seasonality, and competitive position. Ask Quick to audit your ASINs for Q4, and it returns a ranked recommendation built on the seller’s real margin data, historical conversion, and inventory availability. Its recommendations get sharper the more the seller works with it.

## Quick does not stop at your Amazon store

A seller’s business runs across many tools: a storefront on another channel, an email inbox, an accounting system, spreadsheets, a supplier portal. Quick connects to where the seller already works and pulls context from across the whole operation. It is a business assistant that happens to know Amazon deeply, so the seller grows overall, not on one channel alone.

## It turns data into decisions you can act on

Sellers are not short on data. They have Brand Analytics, Search Query Performance, advertising reports, inventory health, and listing quality data. The problem is turning those signals into one clear action. Quick does the synthesis. It watches for change, flags what matters, builds the analysis, and presents the decision with the evidence behind it.

## Your data stays protected on the infrastructure you already sell on

Quick runs on AWS and does not use seller data to train models, not for general improvement and not for anything else. Seller data stays in the seller’s environment and never informs anyone else’s models. For a seller already operating on the Amazon marketplace, there is no new security posture to evaluate. The infrastructure is the same one protecting the seller account today, and the seller’s chosen AWS Region governs where data stays.

# The Amazon Selling Partner plugin

The Selling Partner plugin connects AWS Quick directly to live Seller Central data. After one sign-in with a Seller Central account, the plugin gives conversational access to product listings, inventory, and sales data, pulled live rather than exported and reassembled by hand. It installs like an app, with no coding required. It ships with curated Amazon skills, and it pairs with Amazon Ads MCP so campaign performance and spend sit next to the selling data. The plugin is in beta for US stores.

Figure 1: The Selling Partner plugin consent screen, where the seller grants None, View, or Edit access per data category

The permission screen is worth a pause. The seller grants access per data category, choosing None, View, or Edit for each, and reviews an agreement before authorizing. Nothing is all-or-nothing, and access can be narrowed or revoked later.

# 4. Two use cases a seller can build

At the Accelerate workshop we had sellers build real agents live, from a plain-language description, in a few minutes. Quick turns the description into a working agent, which the seller then refines in conversation.

## Use case A: Inventory Launch Prep

The seller is staging a new product into Amazon and wants the lowest total landed cost. The agent reads supplier data and weighs the fulfillment options per SKU on inbound cost, storage, and speed to sellable. It walks through placement choices and returns the output the seller picks: a shipping plan, a cost comparison, or a combined packet. It explains its reasoning and confirms before it creates anything.

Figure 4: The Inventory Launch Prep agent comparing FBA and AWD fulfillment per factor and recommending a hybrid approach

## Use case B: Post-Launch Product Health

After the launch, the agent reads four signal families through the Selling Partner plugin: listing health, inventory, sales, and traffic. It connects them to find the cause rather than just reporting numbers. Flat sales with healthy traffic points to a conversion problem. Flat sales with falling traffic points to discoverability. Good conversion with falling units points to supply. It returns a health summary per ASIN, the diagnosis with evidence, and a ranked fix list, labeling any low-confidence call.

Figure 5: The Post-Launch Product Health agent’s weekly summary, with week-over-week sales evidence

One honest limit, handled honestly: the selling data does not include cost of goods, so the agent reports margin after Amazon fees and labels it that way. When the seller wants true gross margin, the agent asks them to bring their cost numbers into the conversation. It never presents after-fees margin as if it were gross margin.

# 5. Skills and connectors in action

The Selling Partner plugin and Amazon Ads MCP bring the Amazon selling and advertising data into Quick. Other connectors in AWS Quick bring in the rest of how the business runs, organized by role. These include a second storefront (orders and products from another channel), email and calendar (draft a supplier note, track what is due), team messaging (share an update), and accounting and inventory systems (cost and stock context the selling data does not carry). Because the pieces sit together, the seller can ask one question that reaches across Amazon data and the tools around it.
