# Amazon Polly launches voice cloning: A unique voice for your brand

Voice cloning in Amazon Polly is now generally available as a gated release. Give Amazon Polly a few minutes of audio recordings from a speaker who has agreed to have their voice cloned, and Amazon Polly builds your own cloned voice in that speaker’s voice. The cloned voices run on the Amazon Polly generative engine, which delivers similar naturalness and accuracy as the rest of the generative voices. Amazon Polly voice cloning launches with seven languages: English, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, and Hindi. Each cloned voice speaks the language it was recorded in. The cloned voices are automatically encrypted and access-controlled: only you can access your cloned voices.

Amazon Polly voice cloning is available in all the AWS Regions supported by the Amazon Polly generative engine, including the AWS GovCloud (US) Region. Access is already enabled for select Amazon Polly customers. Others can reach out to their AWS account team or [apply for access](https://pages.awscloud.com/amazon-polly-voice-cloning-20261119.html) to get access.

With this new feature addition, you can build the cloning lifecycle into AWS applications. Building a cloned voice is free for Amazon Polly customers, and Amazon Polly charges for cloned voice usage on a pay-as-you-go basis, see [Amazon Polly Pricing page](https://aws.amazon.com/polly/pricing/) for details.

## What you can build

**A consistent brand voice across channels**

Establish one recognizable voice and use it across your interactive voice response (IVR) system, cascaded voice agents, mobile app, in-store announcements, advertising, and connected devices. Rather than relying on a generic stock voice or re-booking a recording session each time a script changes, you can generate on-brand audio on demand, with a consistent sound in every channel.

**Contact centers**

Deliver a distinctive, on-brand voice across self-service flows, and update prompts as offers and policies change without a new studio session. You can create prompts from source text, test them before rollout, and use the same voice across greeting, routing, and notification experiences.

**Media and content at scale**

Creators, publishers, and studios can create audiobooks, courses, podcasts, game characters, and videos in multiple custom voices to enhance the listening experience, and produce subsequent chapters or updates quickly.

**Localization with a consistent persona**

Maintain a recognizable voice identity across a content library, so narration remains coherent across titles and updates. Each cloned voice speaks only the language it was recorded in. For multilingual experiences, create and review approved voices per supported language.

**Accessibility and personalization**

Offer a natural, human-sounding voice for reading experiences, assistive tools, and personalized interactions. You confirm the speaker’s consent each time you create a voice.

## How voice cloning works

With voice cloning, you can build a custom Amazon Polly voice from recordings of a single speaker and then synthesize speech in that voice through the Amazon Polly generative engine. The voice moves through four steps, from recording the speaker to using the finished voice:

![The voice cloning lifecycle: record, create, wait, use](https://d2908q01vomqb2.cloudfront.net/f1f836cb4ea6efb2a0b1b99f41ad8b103eff4b59/2026/11/19/ML-22432-1.png)

Figure 1: The voice cloning lifecycle, from recording a speaker to using the finished voice

A cloned voice goes through four steps:

- **Record.** You record one speaker (see the following recording tips). You can send the recordings directly in the `CreateVoice` request or upload them on the console. You can also store them in an Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3) bucket in the AWS Region where you want the voice and point Amazon Polly to them.
- **Create.** You call `CreateVoice` with 1-10 recordings, the language they are spoken in, and `TermsAccepted=True`, your confirmation that you have the speaker’s consent. Amazon Polly returns a voice ID (`vc-…`) right away and builds the voice in the background.
- **Wait.** Creation is asynchronous and typically takes 5-10 minutes. During that time, Amazon Polly also checks the quality of your text-to-speech (TTS) voice for speaker similarity. You call `GetVoice` until the status changes from `pending` to `active` or `failed`.
- **Use.** You pass the voice ID to `SynthesizeSpeech` for real-time audio or to `StartSpeechSynthesisTask` for long-form audio written to S3, with `Engine="generative"`, the same way you call the other generative voices in Amazon Polly. When you no longer need the voice, you call `DeleteVoice`.

## Consent and control

A cloned voice is made from one person’s recordings. You confirm that you have the speaker’s consent for all recordings and that you control who can use the voice and when it’s deleted.

- You confirm consent for each voice you create. When you create a voice, on the console or with `TermsAccepted=True` in the API, you confirm that you have obtained all rights and consents necessary to create and use the cloned voice. Your use of voice cloning is subject to the [AWS Service Terms](https://aws.amazon.com/service-terms/), the [Biometric Notice and Consent Terms](https://aws.amazon.com/legal/biometric-notice-and-consent-terms), the [AWS Responsible AI Policy](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/responsible-ai/policy/), and the [AWS Acceptable Use Policy](https://aws.amazon.com/aup/).
- Generated speech carries a watermark. Amazon Polly adds a watermark to text-to-speech audio generated with the cloned voice.
- Only your account can use it. A cloned voice is scoped to the AWS account that created it, and you control access with AWS Identity and Access Management (IAM). You can encrypt voices with your own AWS Key Management Service (AWS KMS) key and tag them.
- You decide when it goes away. Delete a voice with one `DeleteVoice` call or on the console, once it’s active or its creation has failed.
- Amazon Polly does not retain a copy of your voice recordings once the voice is created and doesn’t use them to train Amazon Polly models. It keeps only the voice data it needs to synthesize speech, and stores it encrypted. You can use your own AWS KMS key for that encryption, and `DeleteVoice` removes the data immediately.

We recommend that you:

- Keep a record of each speaker’s consent.
- Delete the voice if the speaker withdraws consent.
- Tell listeners when they’re hearing a synthetic voice where that’s appropriate.
- Grant voice permissions only to the people and systems that need them.

For details on intended uses and limitations, see the [Amazon Polly AI Service Card](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ai/responsible-ai/amazon-polly/overview.html) and the [AWS Responsible Use of AI](https://aws.amazon.com/ai/responsible-ai/) guidance.

## Best practices for recordings

Your cloned voice learns from the recording, including the parts you’d rather it didn’t: the laptop fan, the room echo, the meeting app’s noise filter.

- Record two to ten minutes of natural speech, split into takes of up to a minute. You can send up to 10 recordings per voice, and up to 15 minutes of audio in total. Quality matters more than length: more audio might not change how the voice sounds.
- One speaker, a quiet room, the same distance from the mic. Use a USB microphone, or a phone app that can record WAV. Record in mono WAV, 16-bit, at 24 kHz from the start, so there’s nothing to convert later. Amazon Polly accepts other audio formats, but we recommend WAV. See the [Amazon Polly Developer Guide](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/polly/latest/dg/what-is.html) for supported formats and limits.
- Avoid using meeting apps and browser recorders. Their echo cancellation and noise suppression change the voice, and the clone learns those changes too.
- Record every take in the style you want the voice to have. The cloned voice takes its style from the recordings and can’t blend several, so upbeat, sad, and angry takes won’t give you a voice that can do all three.
- Record in the language the voice should speak. Voice cloning is tested with recordings in US English, German, US Spanish, French, Italian, Brazilian Portuguese, and Hindi. Recordings in other accents of these languages, such as British English or Canadian French, also work but are less tested, so check the voice with your own content.

## Getting started

**On the console:** once voice cloning is enabled for your account (see Availability), expand the left panel and choose **Voice cloning**, upload up to 10 recordings or select them from Amazon S3, confirm the terms, and choose **Create**. Each upload can be up to 5 MB. When the voice is ready, try it out on its details page.

**In code**, you need:

- The latest version of boto3 (`pip install --upgrade boto3`).
- IAM permissions for `polly:CreateVoice`, `polly:GetVoice`, `polly:DescribeVoices`, `polly:SynthesizeSpeech` and `polly:DeleteVoice`. For long-form audio, also `polly:StartSpeechSynthesisTask`, `polly:GetSpeechSynthesisTask` and `s3:PutObject` on the output bucket.
- For the following Amazon S3 example: your recordings in a bucket in the Region where you create the voice, with `s3:GetObject` permission for the IAM role or user that calls `CreateVoice`.

```
import time

import boto3

# Use the Region of your S3 recordings.
polly = boto3.client("polly", region_name="us-east-1")

# 1. Create a voice from recordings of a speaker who has given consent.
#    Use 1-10 clean WAV takes of up to a minute each.
takes = [
    "s3://amzn-s3-demo-bucket/brand-voice/take-1.wav",
    "s3://amzn-s3-demo-bucket/brand-voice/take-2.wav",
    "s3://amzn-s3-demo-bucket/brand-voice/take-3.wav",
]
voice = polly.create_voice(
    Name="MyBrandVoice",    # letters, digits, - and _, up to 64 characters
    LanguageCode="en-US",   # the language spoken in the recordings
    TermsAccepted=True,     # you confirm the speaker's consent and the terms of use
    VoiceCreationData={
        "Recordings": [{"Format": "wav", "Data": {"S3Url": url}} for url in takes],
    },
    Tags={"consent-record": "speaker-0042"},  # optional: the ID of your own consent record, e.g. a signed release
    # Optional: encrypt the voice with your own KMS key (symmetric, same Region).
    # Callers who create or use the voice need kms:DescribeKey,
    # kms:GenerateDataKey and kms:Decrypt on the key.
    # CustomerManagedEncryption={
    #     "KmsKeyArn": "arn:aws:kms:us-east-1:111122223333:key/EXAMPLE"
    # },
)["Voice"]
voice_id = voice["Id"]      # for example "vc-0123456789"

# 2. Check the status every 30 seconds until the voice is active or failed
#    (usually a few minutes). Stop with an error after 20 minutes.
deadline = time.monotonic() + 20 * 60
while voice["Status"] not in ("active", "failed"):
    if time.monotonic() > deadline:
        raise TimeoutError(f"{voice_id} is still {voice['Status']}")
    time.sleep(30)
    voice = polly.get_voice(VoiceId=voice_id)["Voice"]
if voice["Status"] == "failed":
    # Check the recordings, delete this voice and create a new one.
    raise RuntimeError(f"{voice_id}: {voice.get('StatusReason', 'creation failed')}")

# 3. Synthesize speech with your new voice.
speech = polly.synthesize_speech(
    VoiceId=voice_id,
    Engine="generative",
    LanguageCode="en-US",
    OutputFormat="mp3",
    Text="Hello! This is my very own Amazon Polly voice.",
)
with open("my-voice.mp3", "wb") as f:
    f.write(speech["AudioStream"].read())

# When you no longer need the voice:
# polly.delete_voice(VoiceId=voice_id)
```

If Python reports that the `Polly` object has no attribute `create_voice`, upgrade boto3.

For long texts such as audiobook chapters or course modules, use an asynchronous synthesis task that writes the audio to S3:

```
from pathlib import Path

task = polly.start_speech_synthesis_task(
    VoiceId=voice_id,
    Engine="generative",
    LanguageCode="en-US",
    OutputFormat="mp3",
    OutputS3BucketName="amzn-s3-demo-bucket",
    OutputS3KeyPrefix="narration/chapter-01",
    Text=Path("chapter-01.txt").read_text(encoding="utf-8"),
    # SnsTopicArn="arn:aws:sns:us-east-1:111122223333:polly-tasks",  # notify instead of polling
)["SynthesisTask"]
print(task["TaskId"], task["OutputUri"])  # poll get_speech_synthesis_task until "completed" or "failed"
```

## Production considerations

**Create voices ahead of time**

Voice creation takes minutes, so treat it as a provisioning step, not part of a user request. A small workflow (for example, AWS Step Functions or a job queue) can call `CreateVoice`, check `GetVoice` every 30 seconds until the voice is active, and then store the voice ID in your configuration. `CreateVoice` and `DeleteVoice` share a limit of 1 request per second, so queue bulk creations and deletions.

**Keep an inventory**

`DescribeVoices` lists your cloned voices alongside the voices built into Amazon Polly. Cloned voices have `Origin` set to `customer`. Store the voice ID, Region, language, consent reference, and owner for each voice, and give each voice a unique `Name`. If a `CreateVoice` call times out or returns a server error, the voice might already exist, and the SDK’s automatic retries can create a second one. Check `DescribeVoices` for that name before you try again.

**Plan for errors**

`CreateVoice` returns `ThrottlingException` when you exceed the 1-request-per-second limit it shares with `DeleteVoice`, and `ServiceQuotaExceededException` when the account has reached its cloned-voice quota in that Region. Synthesizing with, or deleting, a voice that is still being created returns `ConflictException`. If a voice uses your own KMS key, callers without access to the key get an access-denied error. When creation fails, `StatusReason` gives only a general message, so check the recordings against the preceding recording best practices, then delete the failed voice and create a new one.

**Scope permissions to voices**

Cloned voices are IAM resources, so you can give the role that serves traffic permission to synthesize with one voice of choice. If the voice uses your own KMS key, that role also needs access to the key. Keep `polly:CreateVoice` and `polly:DeleteVoice` for a separate provisioning role. Here’s an example policy for the serving role:

```
{
  "Version": "2012-10-17",
  "Statement": [
    {
      "Effect": "Allow",
      "Action": "polly:SynthesizeSpeech",
      "Resource": "arn:aws:polly:us-east-1:111122223333:voice/vc-0123456789"
    }
  ]
}
```

With tags, you can write the same rule once for a whole group of voices using `aws:ResourceTag` conditions. Review your existing policies too: a statement that allows `polly:SynthesizeSpeech` on all resources (`"Resource": "*"`) also covers every cloned voice in the account.

**Plan for quotas and Regions**

Each account has an initial quota of 10 cloned voices per Region. To request an increase, contact your account team or [AWS Support](https://aws.amazon.com/contact-us/). A voice exists only in the account and Region where you created it. To serve another Region or account, create the voice again in that Region from the same recordings, and make sure the speaker’s consent covers every copy.

**Test with your real scripts**

Before a voice goes live, listen to it reading your own content: product names, numbers, addresses, and the longest sentences you have. The same text can come out slightly differently from one request to the next. For fixed prompts such as phone-line greetings, generate the audio once, approve it with the speaker and your brand team, and serve the stored file. If a name or term comes out wrong, spell it differently in the text or use a Speech Synthesis Markup Language (SSML) `<phoneme>` tag. Phoneme tags work with cloned voices in English, French, Italian, German, and Spanish. See [Supported SSML tags](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/polly/latest/dg/supportedtags.html).
