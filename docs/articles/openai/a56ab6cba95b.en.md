# Team++

We’ve had some fantastic people join over the past few months (and we’re still hiring). Welcome, everyone!

Illustration: Justin Jay Wang × DALL·E


## Full-timers

- **Yura Burda**. [Yura](https://sites.google.com/site/yburda/) finished a math PhD at the age of 24, and switched into machine learning a year and a half ago. He’s focusing on [generative models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_model). He [discovered](http://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00519) a simple but fundamental improvement to the variational lower bound that had evaded notice since its original discovery decades ago.
- **Ian Goodfellow**. [Ian](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ian-goodfellow-b7187213) is well known for his many contributions to machine learning, including the [MaxOut Network](http://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4389) and the [Generative Adversarial Network](http://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2661), the latter of which is a driver of excitement in generative modeling research. In addition, he’s the lead author of the [book](http://www.deeplearningbook.org/) on deep learning.
- **Alec Radford**. [Alec](https://twitter.com/alecrad) created [DCGAN](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06434), a neural model that could generate large, coherent images containing an unprecedented level of global coherence and detail. In addition, his model has learned to do [image analogies](https://github.com/Newmu/dcgan_code#arithmetic-on-faces) in an entirely unsupervised way.
- **Tim Salimans**. [Tim](http://timsalimans.com/) is an expert on variational methods. The author of the first [paper](http://projecteuclid.org/euclid.ba/1386166315) on stochastic gradient variational inference (for which he won the [Lindley prize](https://bayesian.org/awards/LindleyPrize.html)), he was at one point ranked [number 2 overall](https://www.kaggle.com/timsalimans) on Kaggle.

## Interns

Also joining us for the summer (and in some cases, to continue the collaboration once they return to their home institution):

- **Peter Chen**. [Peter](https://www.linkedin.com/in/peter-xi-chen-25178356) previously co-founded a [startup](https://angel.co/sellegit-1) and did research on parallel computing and cognitive science. Recently, he’s been working on reinforcement learning, from theory to applications with deep neural nets.
- **Rocky Duan**. [Rocky](https://www.linkedin.com/in/rocky-duan-1a82662a) is a PhD student who previously co-founded a [startup](https://angel.co/sellegit-1) (with Peter) and worked on a number of projects in robotics. He’s now transitioned to the field of deep reinforcement learning, and recently TA’d Berkeley’s [Deep Reinforcement Learning](http://rll.berkeley.edu/deeprlcourse/) class.
- **Linxi Fan**. Although [Linxi](https://jimfan.me/) is just finishing up his undergrad, he has already worked on a [variety](http://arxiv.org/abs/1511.06430) of projects in deep learning, including [Deep Speech 2](http://arxiv.org/abs/1512.02595).
- **Jon Gauthier**. [Jon](https://twitter.com/j_gauthier) is an undergraduate student whose achievements include, among others, a [method](http://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.06021v1.pdf) that makes it possible to efficiently train large recursive neural networks on large datasets.
- **Jonathan Ho**. [Jonathan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/jonathan-ho-00243623) is a PhD student at Stanford, where he is developing a new approach to imitation learning. Earlier in his research career, he had success [teaching robots how to tie knots](http://rll.berkeley.edu/isrr2013lfd/).
- **Rein Houthooft**. [Rein](http://reinhouthooft.github.io/) hails from Belgium, started his research career in the field of computer networking, and now is developing novel ways to incorporate uncertainty into deep reinforcement learning algorithms.
- **Eric Price**. [Eric](http://www.cs.utexas.edu/~ecprice/) is a professor of theoretical computer science at UT Austin. His achievements include the development and the analysis of a [faster-than-*n log n* sparse Fourier transform](http://dspace.mit.edu/openaccess-disseminate/1721.1/73474). In his past life, he achieved a [perfect score](http://www.ioinformatics.org/locations/ioi05/contest/results.pdf) at the IOI.

As a closing note, we get a lot of questions about what we’re working on, how we work, and what we’re trying to achieve. We’re not being intentionally mysterious; we’ve just been busy launching the organization (and finding awesome people to help us do so!).

We’re currently focused on unsupervised learning and reinforcement learning. We should have interesting results to share over the next month or two. A bunch of us will be around [ICLR](http://www.iclr.cc/), where we’ll likely hold an event of some form. I’ll also host a [Quora Session](https://productupdates.quora.com/Introducing-Sessions) in May or June to answer any questions for people we don’t meet in Puerto Rico.
