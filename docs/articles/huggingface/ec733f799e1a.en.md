## One open model for multilingual transcription, speaker attribution, translation, and audio Q&A, with top leaderboard results, plus the open Granary v2 data pipeline and fine-tuning recipes to adapt it.

A transcript can capture every spoken word and still leave users or applications wondering who said what, how to translate it, or how to format domain-specific jargon. Multilingual calls, fast-paced meetings, and medical dictation each need different output: a translation, speaker labels, or correctly spelled medical terms.

NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe is an open-weight, promptable model that turns recorded speech into useful text, now available on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B) and [ModelScope](https://modelscope.ai/collections/nv-community/Nemotron-35-Transcribe). It brings transcription, speaker attribution (diarization), translation, and audio question answering into one checkpoint: it transcribes 40 languages, translates 31 of them to or from English, and attributes speakers in 13. It handles recordings up to an hour long and labels up to eight speakers. Developers can guide output with natural language, choose where their audio is processed, and adapt recognition to domain vocabulary and accents.

Transcribe is first among open-weight models on the [Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard](https://huggingface.co/spaces/hf-audio/open_asr_leaderboard) for English and sits on [Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/speech-to-text/non-streaming?error-rate=word-error-rate-vs-active-size)’s accuracy–active-parameter frontier. For speaker attribution, it posts the lowest error rate on [David AI’s 21-language diarization leaderboard](https://research.withdavid.ai/blog/dai-asr-i18n#leaderboard) and ranks first in [VoiceArena’s 14-system comparison](https://voicearena.com/diarization-bench/methodology). Quantized versions are coming soon, so you can run Transcribe locally on your RTX Spark, DGX Spark, or Jetson AGX Thor—or scale up on Blackwell data-center GPUs.

Granary v2’s multilingual data (available on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Granary-v2) and [ModelScope](https://modelscope.ai/datasets/nv-community/Granary-v2)) and reusable curation pipeline, together with [NeMo Speech fine-tuning tools](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/tutorials/speechlm2/finetuning/README.md), extend prompt-level control to training: developers can define the written form they need and adapt the model to the speech their applications encounter. In a [medical case study](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/examples/speechlm2/finetuning/docs/medical-asr-synthetic-data.md), fine-tuning cut word error rate (WER) on held-out speakers by 45.8% relative to the best prompt.

# Guide transcription and analysis with natural language

The same checkpoint can turn one meeting recording into different outputs, depending on the instruction:

- “Transcribe this recording and label each speaker.”
- “Translate this audio into English.”
- “What decisions were made in this meeting?”

Experience the model in action in the video below, where it tackles a classic scene from the 1940 screwball comedy His Girl Friday—a film famous for its rapid-fire, heavily overlapping dialogue.

*Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe captions a scene from His Girl Friday (1940) and labels speakers, including overlapping dialogue.*

For unfamiliar terms, try context before training. In a healthcare setting, for example:

`Transcribe this medical discussion in the original language. Possible terms: HbA1c, metformin. Use these terms only when supported by the audio. Preserve standard medical spelling and write numbers in conventional written form.`

The [model card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B) has recommended presets, including reasoning for audio questions, plus the full prompt guide and a task-by-language matrix: language support differs by task, and audio question answering is trained in English. Word-level timestamps are also supported. As with any speech model, spot-check names, speaker changes, and answers against the recording.

**From live classes to lasting lessons.** Agora, whose real-time voice and video platform powers live conversations in apps, used word-level timestamps in a classroom demo: Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe turns a recorded class into a transcript shown alongside the replay, so students can see what was said and when. [Agora’s blog](https://www.agora.io/en/blog/how-live-teaching-can-become-a-lasting-learning-resource/) walks through how they built it.

# Granary v2: The open data curation pipeline behind Transcribe

A promptable speech model requires training data with unambiguous formatting targets. [Granary v2](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Granary-v2) is part of the [Nemotron speech collection](https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/nemotron-speech) on Hugging Face and [ModelScope](https://modelscope.ai/collections/nv-community/Nemotron-Speech). Built with [NVIDIA NeMo Curator](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Curator), its reusable pipeline prepared multilingual speech used in Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe’s training, producing aligned transcript variants, task labels, and inspectable quality metadata. The public manifest covers 463,062 hours across 48 languages and 16 source corpora, up from 25 languages in [Granary v1](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Granary). These figures describe the dataset release, not Transcribe’s full training mixture or its sampling weights.

Granary v2 generates three transcript forms for each recording (Table 1), the same verbatim and clean styles developers can request from Transcribe with a prompt. Beyond transcription, the pipeline produces short audio captions, code-switching restoration of loanwords, speech-grounded Q&A pairs, contextual ASR biasing terms, and translations with automatic quality scores.

| Training target | Illustrative transcript |
| --- | --- |
| Text-normalized, verbatim | I paid, uh, twenty-five euros. |
| Inverse text normalization, with disfluencies | I paid, uh, €25. |
| Inverse text normalization, without disfluencies | I paid €25. |

*Table 1. Granary v2 training targets for the same utterance.*

The pipeline is organized into four reusable stages:

- **1. Audio Preparation (Metadata):** Segments raw or long-form audio, tracks speakers across the full recording, and tags acoustic events and language. Granary v2’s sources were already segmented, so this release skipped this stage.
- **2. Transcription (ASR):** Routes each language to primary and recovery ASR models, falling back when the primary fails a hallucination check. Human reference transcripts help preserve trusted spellings while adding spoken forms and disfluencies.
- **3. Task Label Generation (Text):** Derives transcript variants, then checks character rate, hallucination patterns, and model or human-reference agreement. Only accepted records receive contextual-ASR terms and translations.
- **4. High Fidelity (HiFi):** Filters out segments that are noisy, clipped, or contain multiple speakers, keeping a smaller set of high-quality recordings for uses beyond ASR. For the VoxPopuli dataset, it reduced 354k hours to 111k hours across 22 European languages.

[![fig6-granary-v2-pipeline-redesign Diagram of the Granary v2 curation pipeline following one roughly 12-minute sample from raw audio to a training target. The Metadata pipeline segments the audio with voice activity detection and diarization and identifies the language. The ASR pipeline transcribes a segment with a primary and a fallback model and keeps the best prediction, “Like, Granary-v2 is, uh, making every word matter.” The Text pipeline derives tn_raw, itn_raw, and itn_clean transcripts, plus translations, a caption, a speech Q&A pair, and context-ASR terms. The training view packs these into an instruction pair that maps “Transcribe without disfluencies” to “Granary-v2 is making every word matter.”](https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/68127936de624fb6f57d9989/yORas7atvTqY3x-4BGaCb.png)](https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/68127936de624fb6f57d9989/yORas7atvTqY3x-4BGaCb.png)

*Figure 6. Granary v2 data curation pipeline, following one sample from raw audio through the Metadata, ASR, and Text stages to a training example.*

Developers can run the [NeMo Curator pipeline](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Curator/tree/main/tutorials/audio/granary_v2) with Ray and [NVIDIA Dynamo](https://github.com/ai-dynamo/dynamo) backends on their own recordings or authorized public corpora, or build on the [Granary v2 public manifests](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Granary-v2). The manifests include the three transcript forms as tn_raw, itn_raw, and itn_clean, the labels described above, entity hints, derived instruction pairs, provenance, and a per-record quality flag. Download source audio separately under each corpus’s original license, and follow the release’s published terms.

# Adapt recognition to the vocabulary that matters

Start with a development-set prompt that specifies language, script, and transcript format. In the case study below, naming English and the Latin alphabet in the prompt cut full-benchmark WER from 30.8% to 23.8%. For domain errors that remain, the NeMo Speech fine-tuning workflow pairs representative speech with explicit written targets.

The [NeMo Speech medical ASR case study](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/examples/speechlm2/finetuning/docs/medical-asr-synthetic-data.md) shows this progression on [Eka Care’s open benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ekacare/eka-medical-asr-evaluation-dataset) of recordings from Indian doctors and medical students, which has 3,619 clips and no designated training split. The study builds adaptation data from generated medical text and public medicine catalogs, then combines approximately 153 hours of synthetic speech, 29 hours of accented English from Common Voice, and 63 hours of medical recordings from MultiMed.

[![fig7-eka-care-62pct-rebrand Bar chart titled “62% fewer transcription errors” for the Eka Care medical ASR benchmark of Indian medical speech. On the full test set, word error rate falls from 30.8% out of the box to 23.8% with a better prompt that names English and the Latin alphabet, and to 11.8% after fine-tuning. Side labels note about 14 GPU-hours on one NVIDIA GB300 and about 153 hours of synthetic speech plus 92 hours of public accented and medical speech](https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/68127936de624fb6f57d9989/Dq54q2P9bYJwWZ7prznAT.png)](https://cdn-uploads.huggingface.co/production/uploads/68127936de624fb6f57d9989/Dq54q2P9bYJwWZ7prznAT.png)

*Figure 7. On the full test set of Eka Care’s Indian medical speech benchmark, a better prompt cuts WER from 30.8% to 23.8%, and fine-tuning brings it to 11.8%, about 62% fewer transcription errors than the model out of the box. The full set includes the 855 tuning clips used to choose the prompt and checkpoints; the held-out results below are the cleaner test.*

Synthetic generation separates the written target from its spoken rendering: “HbA1c” is the label, while “H B A one C” is sent to the synthesizer. Multiple TTS voices broaden the audio; real recordings add accents and recording conditions. Training text is filtered for exact matches and shared eight-word sequences with benchmark references.

As with Granary v2’s explicit targets, training examples vary digits, unit spacing, company-name expansion, and brand-name hyphens, with instructions specifying the required convention. Some prompts include term lists with near-miss distractors, so the model learns to use a spelling only when the audio supports it. The final system uses one fixed convention prompt and one model pass, without retrieval or transcript post-processing.

The recipe trains the speech encoder and projection and uses LoRA to adapt the language model. Two runs use different data mixtures; after adapter merging, selected checkpoints are uniformly averaged into one final model. Checkpoint selection uses decoded development-set results, rather than training loss alone. The full adaptation, from text generation through evaluation, took about 14 GPU-hours on one NVIDIA GB300.

## Domain gains, measured on held-out speakers

The study splits the benchmark by speaker into 855 tuning clips, used to choose the prompt and checkpoints, and 2,764 held-out clips, decoded once per final system. Using Eka Care’s [KARMA scoring pipeline](https://github.com/eka-care/KARMA-OpenMedEvalKit), fine-tuning cuts held-out WER from 22.44% for the best-prompt baseline to 12.17%, a 45.8% relative reduction and the clearest evidence that the gains generalize to new speakers. Keyword WER falls from 9.2% to 6.5%, and semantic WER from 10.0% to 7.6%. On the same held-out speakers and scorer, the adapted model’s 12.17% WER beats an open medical-specialist model (13.79%) and a widely used general-purpose ASR model (14.92%).

On general English, LibriSpeech test-clean WER rises slightly, from 1.54% to 1.69%. The medical specialist still leads on exact matches for brand-name drugs and diagnostics; the study points to real recordings of clinicians saying brand names as the next step. For your own domain, keep at least 100 real, labeled clips for checkpoint selection, and evaluate the entity types your application depends on alongside general-speech retention.

The case study includes commands to reproduce each step. The [fine-tuning tutorials](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/tutorials/speechlm2/finetuning/README.md) cover recipes, resource requirements, and tools for training, export, and evaluation, including new-language and code-switching ASR. The accompanying [agent skill](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/.claude/skills/finetune-speech-llm/SKILL.md) lets a coding agent run the workflow, from baseline selection and data checks through training validation, adapter merging, and evaluation.

ModelScope also enables developers to fine-tune Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe through its [ms-swift framework](https://github.com/modelscope/ms-swift) for specialized multimodal applications.

# Get started with Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe and Granary v2

Try Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe in the [Hugging Face demo](https://huggingface.co/spaces/nvidia/nemotron-transcribe) or on [ModelScope Studios](https://modelscope.ai/studios/nv-community/Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe), then download the checkpoint from [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B) or [ModelScope](https://modelscope.ai/collections/nv-community/Nemotron-35-Transcribe). For self-hosted transcription, get started with [vLLM](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/docs/source/speechlm2/vllm_serving.rst) or [SGLang-Omni](https://sgl-project.github.io/sglang-omni/cookbook/nemotron_transcribe.html), both offering support from day one.

To skip self-hosting, use a hosted endpoint from [Baseten](https://www.baseten.co/library/nvidia-nemotron-35-transcribe/), [DeepInfra](https://deepinfra.com/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B), or Simplismart.

To adapt it to your domain, start with the Granary v2 manifests on [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Granary-v2) or [ModelScope](https://modelscope.ai/datasets/nv-community/Granary-v2) and the [medical ASR case study](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/examples/speechlm2/finetuning/docs/medical-asr-synthetic-data.md), or let a coding agent drive the workflow with the [fine-tuning agent skill](https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Speech/blob/main/.claude/skills/finetune-speech-llm/SKILL.md).
